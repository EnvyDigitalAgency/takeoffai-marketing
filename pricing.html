Pricing FAQ

Is the software really free?
Yes. Unlimited projects, unlimited users, manual take-off, exports, your symbol library, price lists and the SimPro connection — all free, permanently, with no card to start. What costs money is the AI doing a take-off for you, because that's the part with a real cost behind it. You can put that on a monthly plan from $149, or skip the plan entirely and buy a pack of credits to spend whenever. Stop paying either way and you keep your account, your projects and your exports.
What am I actually paying for?
A finished take-off, not a tool to do one with. The AI reads the legend itself, works out what needs counting, counts it, and drops a marker on every single item so you can check its work against the drawing. A commercial job replaces six to ten hours of estimator time and costs $50 a take-off on Solo, or $35 on Contractor.
Why can't I just sign up and try it myself?
Every account is tuned to your own symbol legends and drawing standards before it will count accurately — that's the setup we do with you on the onboarding call. A generic self-serve account gives you a poor first take-off and a wrong impression of the tool, so we don't offer one. Book a 30-minute demo and you'll leave with a real BOQ from your own plans, and a free account already tuned to your drawings.
What counts as an "electrical sheet"?
Only the electrical drawings — never pages in the PDF. Upload the entire binder and we work out which sheets are electrical and count just those. On real jobs we've run, a 25-page binder held one electrical sheet. Fifteen electrical sheets is a large tender set, usually a binder of well over a hundred pages.
What's the difference between a residential and a commercial take-off?
Residential is a house or a unit — typically one or two sheets and a short legend. Commercial is offices, retail, industrial, healthcare and the like, where there's far more to read before anything gets counted. Multi-residential and mixed-use sets sit between the two and we quote those individually. Either way you see the exact price for your job before you spend anything.
